**New-phrase convention:** phrases marked 🟡 are NOT part of the current 30-day curriculum — they're natural extensions that preview a future/next-level course. Each is footnoted with `*` and explained below its page.

## Page 1 — Morning Greeting (Market)
*(Days 1, 9 — revised 20 August 2026 for narrative logic: original version had the woman checking a working phone while also asking someone the time, which doesn't make sense. Fixed: her phone has died (0% battery), which is *why* she needs to ask.)*

**Panel 1:** At a Thai market, she greets an elderly woman vendor with a wai.
> สวัสดีค่ะ *(sà-wàt-dii-kâ)*

**Panel 2:** She holds up her phone, showing a dead, 0% battery screen — a rueful, apologetic expression. Since she can't check the time herself, she asks.
> ขอโทษนะคะ กี่โมงแล้วคะ *(khǎaw-thôht-ná-ká, gìi-mohng-láew-ká)*

**Panel 3:** The elderly woman — who has her own smartphone tucked away and a woven market basket on one arm, a nice blend of traditional and modern — answers.
> 🟡 แปดโมงค่ะ *(bpàet-mohng-kâ)*

*🟡 "แปดโมงค่ะ" = "It's eight o'clock." Days 1–30 taught how to **ask** the time, but never how to **answer** it — a natural, high-value next step.*

## Page 2 — Coffee Shop
*(Day 13)*

**Panel 1:** She orders at a small cafe counter.
> เอากาแฟเย็นหนึ่งแก้วค่ะ หวานน้อยนะคะ *(ao-gaa-fae-yen-nèung-gâew-kâ, wǎan-nói-ná-ká)*

**Panel 2:** Barista nods.
> 🟡 ได้ค่ะ *(dâi-kâ)*

*🟡 "ได้ค่ะ" = "Sure / can do." One of the single most common everyday replies in Thai — never explicitly taught, but heard constantly once you start speaking.*

## Page 5 — At the Motorbike Shop
*(Days 6, 15 — plus new question forms not yet in the curriculum)*
**Characters:** a Westerner man, ~28, and a Thai boy, ~19, both customers waiting at a motorbike repair shop

**Panel 1:** The Westerner turns to the boy next to him, curious, makes conversation.
> 🟡 คุณชื่ออะไรครับ *(khun-chêu-à-rai-kráp)*
The boy answers, relaxed.
> ผมชื่อต้อมครับ *(phǒm-chêu-dtôm-kráp)*

*🟡 "คุณชื่ออะไรครับ" = "What's your name?" — Day 15 taught how to **answer** with your name, but never how to **ask** someone else's. The boy's reply correctly uses ผม (younger-speaker form, per LDTKB-040) since he's 19, younger than the 28-year-old man he's speaking to.*

**Panel 2:** The boy, now curious back, asks in return.
> เป็นคนที่ไหนครับ *(bpen-kon-tîi-nǎi-kráp)*
The man answers.
> 🟡 เป็นคนเยอรมันครับ *(bpen-kon-yer-rá-man-kráp)*

*🟡 "เป็นคนเยอรมันครับ" = "I'm German." Day 6 taught how to **ask** where someone's from, but not a natural way to **answer** with a nationality — a direct, useful next step. (Note: "เยอรมัน" is a transliterated loanword — its tones are conventional rather than rule-derived, so this one especially needs native confirmation.)*

**Panel 3:** The man, genuinely interested now, asks about travel.
> 🟡 ชอบไปเที่ยวที่ไหนครับ *(chôrp-bpai-tîeow-tîi-nǎi-kráp)*
The boy answers, pointing vaguely toward the mountains.
> ดอยปุยครับ *(doi-bpuy-kráp)*

*🟡 "ชอบไปเที่ยวที่ไหนครับ" = "Where do you like to travel/visit?" — extends Day 17's "I like..." pattern into a real question about someone else's preferences. ดอยปุย (Doi Pui) is a real, well-known mountain near Chiang Mai — a nice touch of authentic local color rather than an invented place.*
