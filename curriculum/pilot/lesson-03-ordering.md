# Lesson 3 of 7 — Ordering Food & Drink

**Situation:** Ordering food & drink (arc position 3 of 7)
**Phrase taught:** "I would like..." / "Check, please"
**Status:** Draft — requires native-speaker pronunciation review before pilot use

---

## Why this lesson is here (arc rationale)
High-frequency, high-payoff daily situation. Reuses politeness from lesson 1 (krap/kha branching) and sets up quantity language that reuses numbers from lesson 2.

## Delivery sequence (per LDTKB-006)

### Step 1 — Picture
A simple plate of food with a hand gesturing "this one, please" — a recognizable ordering moment.

### Step 2 — Thai Karaoke (gender-branched per LDTKB-024)

| Speaker voice | Karaoke | Tone pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Male | **ao an-níi kráp** | rising – mid – falling – high |
| Female | **ao an-níi kâ** | rising – mid – falling – falling |

*("ao" = want/take, "an-níi" = this one — a flexible, high-utility phrase that works pointing at a menu or a market stall.)*

### Step 3 — English meaning
"I'll take this one" / "I would like this"

### Step 4 — Thai script (decorative)
เอาอันนี้ครับ / เอาอันนี้ค่ะ

### Step 5 — Native audio
Two recordings (male/female branch), matching lesson 1's pattern.

### Step 6 — Activity (recognition tap)
Learner sees 3 short audio clips — one is today's phrase, two are clearly different phrases from lesson 1 (reinforcing prior learning by using them as distractors, not testing new material).

## Open items requiring resolution before pilot-ready
1. Native-speaker pronunciation/tone review.
2. Confirm "ao an-níi" is the best beginner-friendly ordering phrase vs. alternatives (e.g. a more explicit "check, please" phrase) — this is a judgment call worth a native speaker's opinion, not just tone accuracy.
3. Image and audio production method (same open items as lesson 1).
