# Lesson 7 of 7 — Asking for Help / When Things Go Wrong

**Situation:** Asking for help (arc position 7 of 7 — final pilot lesson)
**Phrase taught:** "I don't understand" / "Can you help me?"
**Status:** Pilot-ready — content, native-speaker review, audio, and images all complete (Joule, 19 August 2026)

---

## Why this lesson is here (arc rationale)
The capstone safety-net lesson. Ending the 7-day free pilot on "you can always ask for help" is a deliberate emotional note — reassuring rather than testing, a good close for a free trial that's asking the learner to trust the product enough to consider paying for 30 more days.

## Delivery sequence (per LDTKB-006)

### Step 1 — Picture
A person with a gentle, open "help?" gesture — approachable, not distressed.

### Step 2 — Thai Karaoke (gender-branched per LDTKB-024)

| Speaker voice | Karaoke | Tone pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Male | **mâi kâo-jai kráp** | falling – falling – mid – high |
| Female | **mâi kâo-jai kâ** | falling – falling – mid – falling |

*("mâi kâo-jai" = I don't understand — the single most useful safety-net phrase for a beginner in any real conversation.)*

### Step 3 — English meaning
"I don't understand"

### Step 4 — Thai script (decorative)
ไม่เข้าใจครับ / ไม่เข้าใจค่ะ

### Step 5 — Native audio
Two recordings (male/female branch).

### Step 6 — Activity (recognition tap)
Learner hears today's phrase plus 2 distractors drawn from across the whole week (a light cumulative-review moment, appropriate for a final lesson), taps the one meaning "I don't understand."

### Closing message (pilot-specific, not part of the reusable 6-step template)
A short, warm wrap-up after the activity: acknowledges the 7-day pilot is complete, reassures the learner that "mâi kâo-jai" is always a safe thing to say, and transitions into whatever comes next per Stage 4/8 (free pilot → 30-day paid product) — exact wording is a Stage 7 concern (privacy/payment info must be accessible before this is finalized) and out of scope for this lesson-content batch.
